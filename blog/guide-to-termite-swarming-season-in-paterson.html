The Homeowner's Guide to Termite Swarming Season in Paterson

Recognize the signs of termite swarmers in Paterson, NJ. Learn when swarming season occurs, how to tell termites from ants, and when to inspect.

TTermite Control Paterson ·July 2026 ·8 min read

Every spring, homeowners across Paterson experience a sudden, alarming phenomenon: clouds of small, winged insects emerging inside or around their properties. This event is known as termite swarming, and it is the most visible indicator of a mature termite colony nearby.

If you see these flying pests, it's essential not to panic. Understanding what they are, when they swarm, and how to tell them apart from common ants will help you protect your home effectively.

What Is Termite Swarming Season?

A termite swarm is a mating event. When a subterranean termite colony reaches maturity (usually after 3 to 5 years) and the weather conditions are right, the colony produces "alates" or swarmers. These are reproductive termites equipped with wings, designed to fly out, find a mate, and establish a new sub-colony.

Unlike worker termites, which remain hidden within soil and wood, swarmers emerge into the light. Because they are weak flyers, they often drift with the wind, land on foundations, and quickly shed their wings once they have paired up.

When Does Swarming Happen in Paterson NJ?

In Paterson and throughout northern New Jersey, subterranean termite swarming season typically begins in late March and lasts through June. Swarms are highly weather-dependent and almost always occur during daylight hours under the following conditions:

  • Temperatures rise above 70°F.
  • High humidity levels or immediately after spring rains.
  • Calm, overcast spring mornings.

Being aware of these seasonal triggers allows homeowners to be on high alert during warm, rainy spring weeks.

Swarming Termites vs. Winged Ants: How to Tell the Difference

Flying ants also swarm during spring, leading to frequent cases of mistaken identity. However, treatment methods for ants and termites are completely different. Use this quick reference guide to distinguish them:

  1. Waist Shape: Termites have a broad, straight waist. Ants have a distinct pinched, narrow waist.
  2. Antennae: Termites have straight, bead-like antennae. Ants have bent, elbowed antennae.
  3. Wings: Termite wings are equal in length and shape, extending far past their body. Ant wings have larger front wings and smaller hind wings.

If you find wings or insects, save a few in a jar or clear tape so a technician can identify them under a magnifier.

What Finding Swarmers Indoors Means

If you observe a termite swarm outside in your yard, it indicates termites are active in the local soil. However, finding swarming termites or piles of discarded wings inside your house is a critical warning sign. Because swarmers cannot crawl through solid barriers easily, emerging indoors almost always means they came directly out of the walls, sills, or floorboards, indicating an active infestation inside your home's structural framing.

Common emergence spots include basement windowsills, crawl space hatches, and baseboards in ground-floor rooms.

Next Steps: When to Schedule a Termite Inspection

If you suspect termite activity due to swarmers, take these steps immediately:

  • Do not spray them: Standard bug sprays will kill the flying swarmers but will not affect the thousands of worker termites feeding on wood behind the walls.
  • Collect samples: Save discarded wings or dead insects for identification.
  • Schedule a professional assessment: Contact a licensed specialist for a comprehensive Termite Inspection Paterson NJ. They will map the infestation and recommend targeted soil treatments or baiting systems.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is termite swarming season in New Jersey?
In New Jersey, subterranean termite swarming typically occurs from late March through June, usually on warm, humid days immediately following a rainfall.

How can I tell a flying termite from a flying ant?
Flying termites have straight antennae, equal-sized wings, and a straight, broad waist. Winged ants have bent antennae, front wings larger than hind wings, and a pinched waist.

What does finding discarded wings in my house mean?
Discarded wings near windows or doors are a strong indicator that a termite swarm has occurred inside or very close to your home, suggesting a mature colony is nearby.

Are swarming termites dangerous to touch?
No, swarming termites do not bite or sting humans, and they do not consume structural wood themselves. Their sole purpose is to reproduce and start new colonies.
